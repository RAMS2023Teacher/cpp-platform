# About RoboCode Academy

RoboCode Academy is the website Mr. Perkins's 7th and 8th grade classes use for robotics at Robert Adams Middle School. This page answers the questions families and other teachers ask most.

## Who made this, and how

### Who made RoboCode Academy?

Mr. Perkins, the Digital Literacy and Computer Science teacher at Robert Adams Middle School in
Holliston. He planned the courses, wrote the challenges around his own robot lab (Circuit City Arena),
decided what each lesson teaches, and tests everything with his classes.

### Was it made with AI?

Yes. Mr. Perkins built the site with AI tools, mainly **Claude** from Anthropic. He describes what a
lesson or a feature should do, the AI writes a draft of the lesson text or the code, and he reviews it,
tries it, and asks for changes. Every lesson's simulator steps are also checked by automatic tests
before the site is updated.

He uses AI the way a builder uses power tools: it makes the work faster, but the plan, the judgment and
the responsibility are his. If something in a lesson is wrong, that is his to fix, so please tell him.

### Do students use AI on this site?

No. There is no chatbot and no AI inside the site. Nothing a student types is sent to an AI while they
work. Quizzes are checked by simple rules, and programs run in a simulator in the student's own browser.

### Can other teachers use it?

Yes. The lessons and the code are in a public GitHub repository (RAMS2023Teacher/cpp-platform), with
instructions for setting up your own copy in your own school Google account. Your copy keeps its data
in your Drive; nothing is shared with Mr. Perkins or with other schools.

## Student privacy

### What is the short version?

Privacy came first when this was designed. The site runs inside the school's own Google Workspace, and
student work is saved in the teacher's school Google Drive, the same place as Google Classroom work.
There are no ads, no tracking, no outside accounts and no company that receives student records.

### How do students sign in?

With the school Google account they already use. There is no separate username or password, and the
site only opens for people signed in to a Holliston school account. It cannot be opened from a personal
Google account.

### What does the site save about a student?

Only what the class needs:

- their school email address and the name on the class list (or the name they typed when joining)
- which class they are in
- their lesson progress: boxes ticked, answers to lesson questions, quiz answers and scores, the
  programs they wrote, and their reflections
- game items: points (XP), badges, their robot avatar, arcade scores
- their arena team's name, mascot and motto
- feedback they chose to give: "this was confusing" flags, lesson ratings and any comment they typed
- a help request ("I'm stuck") and its note, kept for up to an hour

It does **not** save grades from other classes, addresses, birthdays, photos, voice, location or
anything from the student's computer.

### Where is it saved?

In a folder in the teacher's school Google Drive, as one small file per student plus one settings file
for classes, teams and scores. A daily backup is kept in the same folder. Holliston's Google Workspace
account holds it, under the same protections as the rest of students' school Google work.

### Who can see a student's work?

The student and their teacher. A partner the student chooses for a lesson shares that lesson's work.
Classmates can see first names, robot avatars, team names and scores on the leaderboards and the class
display. Classmates never see each other's email addresses, answers or programs.

### Is anything sent to other companies?

No student records are. To be exact about what a student's browser loads from outside the school:

- **Fonts** and small icon pictures come from Google's font service.
- **Pictures of VEX parts** in some lessons load straight from VEX's own website.
- **VEXcode IQ**, VEX's free coding tool, opens in its own tab when a student sends a program to a real
  robot. That is VEX's website, not this one, and students do not sign in to it.
- **CAD Studio**, Mr. Perkins's 3D building site, also opens in its own tab. It runs in the same school
  Google account.
- A lesson **video**, if the teacher adds one, plays from Google Drive, YouTube (in its privacy mode,
  without tracking cookies) or Vimeo.
- If the teacher imports a class list, it is read from **Google Classroom**, inside the same school
  Google account.

Loading a font or a picture tells that website only what any web page visit does (such as an internet
address), not who the student is. The site has no advertising, analytics or tracking code.

### How long is it kept, and can it be deleted?

Student files stay in the teacher's Drive until he deletes them. Mr. Perkins can remove a student from a
class at any time, and can delete a student's file on request. Families who want to see what is saved
for their child, or want it removed, can ask him.

## How it is used in class

### What do students actually do?

They pick a ready-built VEX IQ robot chassis, change and tune it, and write real C++ to control it. Each
lesson has a mission, building steps with a code editor and a robot simulator, a short quiz, and a
turn-in. Programs that work in the simulator are then run on a real robot.

### What is Circuit City Arena?

The class's robot competitions: challenges such as the Impossible Bridge, Castle Crasher, Sumo and Maze
Runner. Students form teams with a name, a mascot and a logo, and earn points for their team. The
leaderboard on the classroom screen shows team names and first names only.

### Why are there points, badges and a game?

To make practice feel worth doing. Points (XP) come only from schoolwork: finishing steps, quizzes and
lessons. They can be spent on robot avatar parts and on a short arcade game that only opens when the
teacher opens it. Nothing costs money, and there is nothing to buy.

### Can students use it at home?

Yes, on any computer with Chrome, signed in to their school Google account. The lessons, the editor and
the simulator all work at home. Only the steps with a real robot need the classroom.

### Is this how students are graded?

The site shows the teacher each student's progress, answers and programs, which he uses along with
what he sees in class. Points and badges are for motivation and are not grades.

## Questions or concerns

### Who do I ask?

Mr. Perkins, at Robert Adams Middle School. He is glad to show families the site, what is saved for
their child, and how a lesson works.
